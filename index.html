A Comment on: “Expected Uncertain Utility”

Simon Grant, Shuo Li Liu, Jingni Yang

Econometrica 92(1), 247–256

Paper ↗PDF ↗
Summary

This comment revisits the axiomatic foundations of Gul and Pesendorfer’s expected uncertain utility model. It gives a counterexample to the original characterization and establishes a corrected representation theorem by strengthening the conditions on diffuse bets and continuity. The revised conditions ensure that ideal events form a sigma-algebra and support a well-defined interval utility.

Published research article2025

Convexity under ambiguity

Xueqi Dong, Shuo Li Liu

Economic Theory Bulletin 13, 353–373

Paper ↗PDF ↗
Abstract

This paper formally defines the concept of preference convexity in a portfolio choice problem under ambiguity, where financial assets are modeled as standard Anscombe–Aumann acts. We provide a condition for its existence: preferences that are mean-preserving spread averse on the lottery space and satisfy the Uncertainty Aversion Axiom (Schmeidler 1989) on the act space. This result offers a foundation for diversification under ambiguity, without assuming concavity of utility functions.

Workshop poster2026

Savage Without Monotonicity

Shuo Li Liu, Jingni Yang

Philosophy Meets Machine Learning: What Counts As Trustworthy? (PhilML), ICML 2026 workshop

Paper ↗PDF ↗
Abstract

Savage separates belief from taste: coherent preferences over acts can be represented by subjective probabilities over states and utilities over consequences. We identify a conditional boundary of that separation. Savage's full-domain ideal ranges over both state-contingent acts and events, and its representation delivers a finitely additive probability. Those rich infinite domains are not added here as a special counterexample; they are the standard Savage-style machinery that lets preference data separate probabilistic belief from utility. Under either a countable convex-range route or a constructibility-based null-ideal route, that package can supply a positive event \(A\) partitioned into disjoint null cells \(E_n\). On a real-valued outcome scale, dominance supplies strictly better consequences whose utility gains tend to zero. Expected utility can then tie an act with its statewise improvement: dominance says the improved act is strictly preferred because the improvement occurs throughout a behaviorally non-null event, while representation says the acts are indifferent. The contribution is a philosophical premise separation, not a claim that finitely additive pathologies themselves are new: representation, dominance, and the repairs that connect them must be distinguished. Countable additivity, an explicit dominance axiom, act-domain restrictions, consequence restrictions, or state-space restrictions block the construction. Disentangling belief and taste does not itself supply monotonicity.

Workshop poster2026

Behavior-Only Deployment Certification Requires Bridge Assumptions

Shuo Li Liu

Failure Modes in Agentic AI (FAGEN), ICML 2026 workshop

Paper ↗PDF ↗
Abstract

A model can pass every behavioral evaluation an auditor runs and still fail a deployment safety claim, not because the auditor made a statistical mistake, but because the evaluation never touched the histories on which the claim depends. We formalize this failure mode for agentic systems. A fixed evaluator \(Q\) observes transcripts on an evaluation-induced support and asks whether a deployment-risk property \(R_{\rm deploy}(\pi)\le r\) holds. If deployment contains a bad set \(B\) outside that support and the admissible policy class permits off-support variation, two policies can induce the same transcript law under \(Q\) while disagreeing on the deployment property. One policy is safe; the other copies it on every evaluator-reachable history and fails on \(B\). If \(B\) is merely rare rather than unreachable, constant-power behavior-only detection requires \(\Omega(1/\varepsilon)\) episodes when the evaluator reaches \(B\) with probability \(\varepsilon\). Behavioral tests, protocol-visible logs, red-team transcripts, and preference scores remain useful for debugging and distribution-scoped risk estimation. They become deployment certificates only when support expansion, structural restrictions, trusted non-behavioral evidence, or other bridge assumptions link evaluation histories to deployment histories.

Workshop poster2026

Model Collapse Despite Ground-Truth Verification

Shuo Li Liu

Workshop on Hypothesis Testing, ICML 2026

Paper ↗PDF ↗
Abstract

Exact verifier labels do not stop a sampled source from losing target support. A proof checker can accept every retained artifact under its declared manifest while leaving target modes that never reach the verifier untested. Lean answers one local question: did this artifact check in the declared environment? It checks the surviving artifacts; it cannot restore target support removed by generation or filtering. We formalize this support-loss form of collapse for proof-supervised LLMs. Lean acceptance is exact for a sampled formal artifact; it is not a claim that the artifact distribution equals the target population. A target-risk claim concerns tasks $x\sim P$ in a different space $\mathcal{X}$. We therefore separate artifact checks from target-task risk. A target-risk report must declare an adapter and audit it over target modes. Our first result shows the cost of missing support: if a target mode has positive mass but zero anchored source mass, two systems can have the same verifier statistics while their target risks differ by that mode's mass. The verifier has not failed; the denominator has changed. Our fixed-model bound charges residual mass, unsupported target mass, and, on supported modes, verified source loss, loss-class discrepancy, formalization error, and holdout uncertainty. For hypothesis testing, a verified aggregate becomes evidence about $P$ only after the report declares the tested population $P$, $H_0$, $H_1$, the test level $\eta$, and the upper-bound statistic $T_{\mathrm{audit}}$. The rejection rule is then explicit, for example $T_{\mathrm{audit}}\le\epsilon$.

Workshop poster2026

Bayesian Truth Serum for LLMs?

Shuo Li Liu

Statistical Frameworks for Uncertainty in Agentic Systems (AgenticUQ), ICML 2026 workshop

Paper ↗PDF ↗
Abstract

Bayesian Truth Serum (BTS) asks each respondent for an answer and a forecast of how other respondents will answer. For strategic human respondents, the scoring rule can reward truthful reporting at equilibrium. We test whether this signal helps when the respondents are language models serving as reviewers, judges, and monitors. On 871 code and reasoning review items judged by four GPT-5-variant models, every BTS-family rule is less accurate than majority vote. The main BTS rule reaches 0.9724 accuracy versus 0.9793 for majority vote, with paired McNemar p=0.031, and other BTS/SP losses survive Benjamini-Hochberg correction. A 150-item MMLU, TruthfulQA, and ARC Challenge probe shows the same pooled ordering. Calibration moves in the opposite direction: BTS-family rules reduce binned expected calibration error by 21-32 percent, and the Surprisingly Popular rule has the lowest split-conformal coverage-error diagnostic. The result is structural. LLMs are not strategic agents, share training and post-training signals, and emit meta-predictions as prompted decoder samples rather than coherent private posteriors. BTS should therefore be treated as a calibrated routing or abstention score inside a distribution-free uncertainty layer, not as a default replacement for majority voting.

Working paper2025

Clarifications and Extensions to “Regret Theory: A New Foundation.”

Enrico Diecidue, Songyu He, Shuo Li Liu, Jeeva Somasundaram

SSRN 5356990

Paper ↗Earlier note (2024) ↗Earlier note (2023) ↗
Abstract

Diecidue and Somasundaram (2017) provides an axiomatic foundation for regret theory (Loomes and Sugden, 1982). This note presents counterexamples that show that their axioms are insufficient. We provide appropriate axioms that characterize regret theory. In addition, we provide axiomatic foundations for regret theory generalizations proposed by Loomes and Sugden (1987) and Bikhchandani and Segal (2011). Our axioms define and identify clear boundaries between increasingly general versions of regret theory.

Counterexamples to “Transitive Regret”

Yuan Chang, Shuo Li Liu

arXiv:2407.00055

Paper ↗PDF ↗
Abstract

Theorem 1 in Bikhchandani & Segal (2011; Theoretical Economics) suggests that a complete, transitive, monotonic, and continuous preference is regret based if and only if it is expected utility. Their Proposition 1 suggests that transitivity and continuity of a regret-based preference implies an equivalence condition: if random variables X and Y have the same distribution, then X ∼ Y. We give counterexamples to Proposition 1.

Working paper2023

Can Regret be Rational?

Shuo Li Liu

SSRN 4437540

Paper ↗Related earlier paper (2022) ↗
Abstract

Under uncertainty, regret is a function depending on both the realized outcome and forgone outcome on a state. This paper studies a general and simple model that only regret-based decisions are assumed without other restrictions such as additive separability, the existence of objective probability, monotonicity, weak comparative probability, and the existence of infinitely divisible non-atomic events (Bell 1982; Loomes and Sugden 1982; Fishburn 1987; Sugden 1993). Counter-intuitively, Savage's P2 is the behavioral axiom equivalent to the existence of a regret function. Without additional assumptions, regret-based behavior is compatible with rationality. The compatibility between regret and rationality is further examined under regularity conditions such as continuity and monotonicity.

Working paper2026

AI Labor Rubric: A Claim Ledger for AI Research Labor

Shuo Li Liu

Working paper

Paper ↗PDF ↗
Abstract

Multi-agent research systems are often evaluated by visible activity: agent count, generated files, debate rounds, consensus, or apparent task completion. These signals can overstate useful AI labor when they hide duplication, verification cost, weak evidence, or unused artifacts. We introduce the AI Labor Rubric (ALR), a preregistration-ready evaluation ledger for measuring whether agentic work creates verifier-adjudicated marginal value under explicit budget, role, and evidence constraints. ALR records base audit rows for accepted, rejected, unresolved, and cost-only work, plus optional contribution-estimate fields when a leave-one-out, replacement, or Shapley-style intervention is actually run. We instantiate ALR on anonymized case-study telemetry: a 650-row labor panel spanning 13 anonymized workflow instances and 50 timestamps, a focal organizational trace with 1,158 recorded calls, and a small controlled agent-pipeline pilot on a hard MMLU-Pro slice. The telemetry motivates the need for the ledger: high output volume and spend do not imply verified progress. The pilot is correspondingly modest: a verifier-gated hierarchy arm reaches 8/12, while two matched-call baselines reach 5/12 and 4/12, with exact sign-test $p=0.25$. The contribution is an evaluation methodology and artifact schema for auditing AI research labor by verifier-adjudicated value rather than visible activity metrics, with workflow choice cast as a matched-budget protocol-selection problem rather than a claim about scale.

Working paper2026

What Is Left for Human Researchers? Coverage Profiling Against Support Collapse in Agentic Drug Discovery

Shuo Li Liu

Working paper

Paper ↗PDF ↗
Abstract

What is left for human researchers when drug discovery agents can propose molecules, optimize leads, and run closed-loop assays? We argue that the answer is an information constraint. Valid assays give ground truth only on sampled support: a lead-optimization agent LO can become excellent within a familiar scaffold series while losing evidence on a resistance mechanism, toxicity channel, or patient subgroup that later determines clinical value. We model two firms competing on one disease and introduce a coverage-profiling program CP that deliberately measures undercovered scaffolds, mechanisms, toxicity channels, subgroups, and assay regimes. The key wedge is that low LO error on induced data can coexist with unidentified target-relevant modes. CP is valuable because it changes future training support. Human researchers enter only through the same channel: they are valuable when they supply non-correlated coverage evidence, such as mode definitions, assay choices, failure interpretation, and validation outside the model's proposal stream. Consequently, as automation costs fall, human demand can first decrease and then increase: cheap LO substitutes for routine optimization, while cheaper CP expands the scale of coverage decisions where non-correlated human input has positive marginal value.

Working paper2026

Private AI, Public Answer Work, and Certification at Stack Overflow

Shuo Li Liu

Working paper

Paper ↗PDF ↗Replication ↗
Abstract

Private AI creates a new outside option for technical problem solving. Its effects on public knowledge platforms may therefore appear less in activity counts than in which questions still reach the public archive, who performs visible answer work inside that residual queue, and how that work becomes certified public resolution.

We analyze the official Stack Overflow 2025Q4 dump: 2,035,885 single-focal-tag questions and 2,391,883 matched answers across 16 technical domains from 2020-01-01 to 2025-12-31. The design compares domains where early GenAI was a more plausible substitute.

We find reallocation inside a residualized public queue, not just decline. In more exposed domains, the remaining public questions become more context-heavy and less exposure-heavy. Within that queue, recent entrants become more visible in first-answer and early-endorsement roles than in accepted-answer certification roles. A certification-conversion ladder shows that first answers, first-positive answers, and top-scored answers are less likely to become accepted within the first week or month in more exposed domains after the transition. Tag-months with larger early-versus-accepted composition gaps exhibit slower endorsed resolution and weaker accepted-answer outcomes. In a strict question-side AI-ban timing test around 2022-12-05, accepted-window certification weakens more clearly than immediate answer arrival. External and direct-AI validation layers are consistent with this interpretation.

The central implication is that private AI can leave a public platform active while residualizing the public queue, thinning incumbent answer capacity, reallocating visible answer work toward earlier roles, and weakening the link between early response and later public certification.

Published conference abstract2020

Patent Collateralization: The Effect of Policy and Impact on Patenting

Meijun Liu, Shuo Li Liu, Yuandi Wang, Richard Freeman

Academy of Management Proceedings 2020(1), 20539

Paper ↗
Abstract

Pledging patents as loan collateral has increased in advanced countries with the growth of intangibles in the value of firms. Despite China's less sophisticated market in intangible capital, collateralization has also increased in China. Using evidence on city-level policies to encourage collateralization in the early 2000s, we find that government efforts to encourage the practice sparked much of China's increase in patent collateralization from 2002 to 2015. Bank lenders – a group more connected to the government than non-bank financial institutions – favored older, larger, private enterprises. The most effective policies provided direct financial support to firms and lending institutions. Policies worked best in cities where intellectual property rights were better protected. By adding value to patents, patent pledge policies induced firms to make more patent applications, potentially encouraging innovation. The impact of government policy on pledging patents as loan collateral reflects the state's continuing entrepreneurial role in capitalism with Chinese characteristics.
